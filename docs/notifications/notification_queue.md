**Document:** `notification_queue.md`  
**Product:** SalesGenie / FlowMind AI

## 1. Purpose

The Notification Queue subsystem manages the reliable, scalable, prioritized, observable, and policy-aware delivery of notifications generated by SalesGenie.

The subsystem shall decouple notification producers from notification delivery providers through durable queues, workers, routing policies, retry mechanisms, dead-letter queues, scheduling, rate limiting, deduplication, idempotency, tenant isolation, AI-assisted prioritization, and human-controlled operational workflows.

The system shall support notifications originating from:

- AI agents
- Human users
- Customer conversations
- Lead intelligence
- CRM events
- Workflow automation
- Sales activities
- Support activities
- Security events
- Billing events
- Compliance events
- System monitoring
- Analytics and predictive analytics
- Administrative operations
- Scheduled campaigns
- External integrations

Supported notification channels shall include:

- Email
- SMS
- Push notifications
- In-app notifications
- Webhooks
- Microsoft Teams
- Slack
- WhatsApp where enabled
- Future enterprise channels through pluggable adapters

## 3. System Requirements

## SR-001 — Queue Architecture

The platform shall implement a durable asynchronous notification queue architecture.

The architecture shall support:

* Horizontal scaling
* Partitioning
* Consumer groups
* Priority queues
* Delayed queues
* Scheduled queues
* Retry queues
* Dead-letter queues
* Tenant-aware queues
* Channel-specific queues

---

## SR-002 — Durable Message Storage

Notification jobs shall be durably persisted before acknowledgement to upstream producers.

The system shall prevent notification loss during:

* Worker crashes
* Service restarts
* Network failures
* Provider failures
* Database failures
* Queue broker failures

---

## SR-003 — At-Least-Once Processing

The notification system shall support at-least-once processing semantics.

All notification handlers shall therefore be idempotent.

---

## SR-004 — Exactly-Once Business Effect

Where technically possible, the system shall guarantee exactly-once business effects through:

* Idempotency keys
* Transactional outbox
* Deduplication
* Provider message identifiers
* Delivery-state reconciliation

---

## SR-005 — Queue Partitioning

Queue workloads shall support partitioning by:

* Tenant
* Channel
* Priority
* Region
* Notification type
* Provider

Partitioning shall prevent a single tenant or workload from monopolizing shared infrastructure.

---

## SR-006 — Priority Scheduling

The queue engine shall support priority-aware scheduling.

Critical notifications shall be processed ahead of lower-priority notifications subject to:

* Fairness policies
* Tenant quotas
* Channel constraints
* Provider limits
* Regulatory constraints

---

## SR-007 — Fair Scheduling

The queue engine shall implement fairness mechanisms such as:

* Weighted fair queuing
* Tenant quotas
* Per-recipient throttling
* Per-channel quotas
* Priority aging

The system shall prevent starvation of lower-priority jobs.

---

## SR-008 — Horizontal Scalability

Notification workers shall scale horizontally based on:

* Queue depth
* Processing latency
* CPU
* Memory
* Provider throughput
* Consumer lag
* Notification arrival rate

---

## SR-009 — Backpressure

The system shall implement backpressure when downstream systems cannot process additional notifications.

Backpressure shall prevent:

* Queue explosion
* Worker overload
* Provider overload
* Retry storms
* Database saturation

---

## SR-010 — Rate Limiting

The platform shall enforce rate limits at:

* Tenant level
* User level
* Recipient level
* Channel level
* Provider level
* Campaign level
* Workflow level
* AI agent level

---

## SR-011 — Provider Quotas

Provider-specific limits shall be dynamically enforced.

The system shall track:

* Requests per second
* Requests per minute
* Daily quota
* Monthly quota
* Provider-specific restrictions

---

## SR-012 — Retry Architecture

The system shall support configurable retries using:

* Exponential backoff
* Jitter
* Maximum attempts
* Maximum elapsed time
* Error classification
* Provider-specific retry policies

---

## SR-013 — Error Classification

Failures shall be classified as:

* Transient
* Permanent
* Rate-limited
* Authentication
* Authorization
* Validation
* Provider outage
* Network
* Timeout
* Recipient unavailable
* Policy violation
* Security block

Only retryable errors shall automatically enter retry processing.

---

## SR-014 — Dead-Letter Queue

Notifications that cannot be successfully processed shall be moved to a durable dead-letter queue.

DLQ messages shall preserve:

* Original payload
* Original metadata
* Error history
* Retry history
* Timestamps
* Provider responses
* Trace IDs
* Tenant context

---

## SR-015 — Delayed Queue

The platform shall support delayed notification execution.

Delayed jobs shall survive:

* Worker restart
* Queue restart
* Service restart
* Infrastructure failure

---

## SR-016 — Scheduled Queue

Scheduled notifications shall use persistent scheduling rather than relying exclusively on in-memory timers.

---

## SR-017 — Transactional Outbox

Services generating notifications shall support transactional event publication through an outbox pattern where appropriate.

The system shall prevent:

```text

```

## 4. Functional Requirements

## FR-001 — Create Notification Job

The system shall provide an API for creating notification jobs.

```http
POST /api/v1/notifications
```

The API shall:

1. Authenticate the caller.
2. Authorize notification creation.
3. Validate tenant context.
4. Validate recipient.
5. Validate channel.
6. Validate payload.
7. Apply notification policies.
8. Generate notification ID.
9. Generate or validate idempotency key.
10. Persist the job.
11. Enqueue the job.
12. Return queue status.

## 10. Core Design Principles

1. **Durability over convenience**
2. **Idempotency by default**
3. **At-least-once processing with exactly-once business effects where achievable**
4. **No cross-tenant notification leakage**
5. **Policy before delivery**
6. **Human authority over high-risk AI decisions**
7. **AI assists; deterministic controls enforce**
8. **Failures must be isolated**
9. **Retries must be bounded**
10. **Dead-letter queues must be actionable**
11. **Provider failures must not cascade**
12. **Every privileged operation must be auditable**
13. **Sensitive data must be minimized**
14. **Queue operations must be observable**
15. **Notification delivery must be resilient**
16. **The system must scale horizontally**
17. **Every external delivery must be idempotent**
18. **Consent and preference state must be revalidated where required**
19. **Autonomous AI operations require explicit guardrails**
20. **Human intervention must always remain possible**
